Abstract
The synthesis of superheavy elements (\(Z \ge 114\)) via heavy-ion fusion reactions is severely bottlenecked by intense prompt quasifission competition in the entrance channel and highly restrictive survival probabilities during compound nucleus de-excitation. While conventional macroscopic-microscopic models treat barrier penetration, compound nucleus formation (\(P_{\text{CN}}\)), and statistical survival (\(W_{\text{sur}}\)) as sequentially isolated processes, we present a unified phenomenological framework: the Nuclear Extension of the Farid Wave Phase-Locking Slot Model.
In this formulation, a nuclear "slot" is defined operationally as an unoccupied, Pauli-compatible single-particle state or time-reversed Kramers pair near the evolving Fermi surface of the dinuclear system, isolated via a projection operator within an energy window \(\Delta _{\text{slot}}\). The Farid Golden Lock Function (\(\mathcal{L}_{\text{FGL}}\)) is introduced as a multi-condition quantum-matching functional that quantifies the simultaneous conservation and alignment of energy, angular momentum, parity, state occupancy, and deformation-dependent Nilsson quantum numbers \([N, n_z, \Lambda, \Omega]\).
We demonstrate how the optimization of this phase-locking functional—the Farid Golden Lock—theoretically modulates the microscopic fusion probability \(P_{\text{CN}}\), offering a structural mechanism to redistribute capture yields into highly stable, shell-corrected states near the predicted island of stability (\(N \approx 184\)). Crucially, the framework maintains strict theoretical firewalls against macroscopic or non-nuclear energy scales, ensuring direct experimental falsifiability through state-selective excitation functions and polarization-dependent residue yields.

1. Introduction
The experimental synthesis of superheavy elements is among the most demanding problems in nuclear physics. The production cross-sections (\(\sigma _{\text{ER}}\)) for elements approaching the nominal island of stability (\(Z = 114, 120, 126\); \(N = 184\)) drop precipitously into the picobarn and femtobarn regimes. The primary theoretical obstacle lies within the dynamical evolution of the fused system: once the projectile penetrates the entrance-channel Coulomb barrier, the collective mass-asymmetry degrees of freedom often drive the dinuclear system toward prompt fission-like re-separation (quasifission) before a true compound nucleus can equilibrate.
Traditional reaction theory typically addresses this multi-stage problem by decoupling the kinematics into independent steps:
\(\sigma _{\text{ER}}(E_{\text{cm}})=\frac{\hbar ^{2}}{2\mu E_{\text{cm}}}\sum _{J=0}^{\infty }(2J+1)T_{J}(E_{\text{cm}})P_{\text{CN}}(E_{\text{cm}},J)W_{\text{sur}}(E^{*},J)\quad \text{\ [0.1.6,\ 0.1.8]}\)
The purpose of the present work is to introduce an organizational, microscopic extension of the Farid Wave Phase-Locking Slot Model to these subatomic systems. The central hypothesis of this theme is that successful heavy-ion synthesis can be optimized by treating the transition from a dinuclear configuration to an equilibrated compound system as a synchronized multi-condition matching problem. Rather than relying on chaotic statistical thermalization, the model investigates whether the deliberate matching of incoming valence nucleon states to empty, deformation-dependent single-particle slots near the Fermi surface can fundamentally suppress quasifission pathways.
